Aliso Viejo became Orange County's 34th city on July 1, 2001, and it has about 52,176 residents in 6.93 square miles. Its housing is newer than most neighbors, which changes the kind of water damage we see.

Storm season and the October to April window

The city's local hazard mitigation plan rates flooding a medium threat, citing October through April rain and hillside topography. Aliso Creek runs through the city, and federal disaster declarations for the severe winter storms of 2023 are listed in the plan as affecting Aliso Viejo.

Rainfall normals at the nearest airport station run about 2.6 inches in January and February and 1.6 inches in March. If a roof or window seal fails, that is when water enters, so inspect flashing and caulk before October and not after the first storm.

Newer housing, different failures

The Mission Viejo Company bought the last 6,600 acres here in 1976, the county approved the master plan in 1979 and the first homes went on sale in March 1982. The city's own plans describe the stock as relatively newer, and growth reached buildout soon after incorporation.

Newer homes mean fewer original-pipe failures and more appliance, supply-line and water heater leaks, along with window and roof flashing issues. In multi-story buildings, a leak upstairs often reaches two units, so fast emergency water removal limits the spread.
