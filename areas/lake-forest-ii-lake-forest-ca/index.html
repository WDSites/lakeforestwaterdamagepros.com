A planned lake community from 1970

GuideStar describes Lake Forest II as a private planned community of 3,436 homes, founded in 1970. It maintains a lake, greenbelts, parks and recreation facilities, and the Sun and Sail Club is its center. A homeowners association law firm describes the lake as 36 acres.

Wikipedia says condominiums and custom homes line two man-made lakes in Lake Forest, with Lake II being the Sun and Sail Club. The same article says a late 1960s residential development was built among the old eucalyptus groves.

Attached homes and shared walls

Where homes share walls, floors or ceilings, a leak in one unit can wet another. If you rent or own an attached home, tell the association manager and your neighbor early, and keep a dated record of what you saw.

We cannot tell you who is responsible. That depends on your association's governing documents, the cause and your policy. We can dry the affected rooms and write down what we measured and removed.

The lake and flood questions

Lake Forest's December 2021 climate vulnerability assessment lists the city's lakes, along with Aliso, Serrano and San Diego Creeks and Borrego Canyon Wash, among places with mapped 100-year and 500-year flood areas. It also says only a small part of the city is mapped.

Older systems and what we check

Homes in a 1970 community may have been updated many times, so we do not assume what is behind a wall. We measure moisture first and report what we find, and we advise a plumber when the repair is a pipe or a valve rather than the water itself.

Pre-1978 homes may need lead-safe work practices, and ceiling coatings from 1945 to 1978 may contain asbestos. A registered abatement contractor handles regulated materials, and we tell you if a test should come first.

Insurance paperwork

A written scope, photos and moisture readings can help you describe the loss. Your insurer decides coverage, and association master policies and your own policy may divide responsibility, so ask both questions.
