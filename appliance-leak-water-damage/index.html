Most appliance leaks are small at first. A cracked hose drips for weeks, and the damage shows up later as a swollen floor edge or a musty cabinet.

Where appliance water actually ends up

Water from an appliance rarely stays on the surface. It runs under the unit, wicks into the baseboard and flows along the subfloor to the lowest point. A washer in an upstairs laundry closet can send water through the floor into the ceiling of the room below.

Laundry closets, kitchen alcoves and garage utility corners are the usual starting points. The wet area is often larger than the puddle suggests.

Safety steps for the first hour

  1. Close the appliance shutoff valve. Washers have two valves behind the unit, and dishwashers and ice-makers have one under the sink or behind the refrigerator.
  2. Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and the floor around it are dry. Otherwise leave it and shut off that circuit at the panel.
  3. Move rugs, boxes and small items off the wet area, and mop standing water from hard floors.
  4. Take photos of the leak, the appliance label and the damage before anything is moved.

If you cannot find the valve, your main water shutoff is the fallback. Your water bill shows whether Irvine Ranch Water District or El Toro Water District serves your address, and either one can tell you where your main shutoff is located.

How the water category changes the plan

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard that sorts water into three categories. A clean supply line or ice-maker line is Category 1. Washing machine and dishwasher discharge is Category 2, because it carries detergent, soil and bacteria from the drain side.

That difference matters for carpet and pad, which can often be dried when the source was a clean supply line but are usually removed after Category 2 water. Hard flooring is cleaned and dried either way. A water that sat for days can be reclassified as it degrades.

Our process on an appliance loss

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera map wet flooring, baseboard and the cabinet or wall behind the unit.

Portable extraction units pull standing water from floors and carpet, and wet pad is removed when the category calls for it.

Insurance usually asks one question first: was the leak sudden and accidental, or gradual? Many homeowners policies cover a burst hose or an overflow, while a leak that dripped for months from a worn fitting is often treated as maintenance. Check your policy, report the loss right away and keep your receipts.

Mistakes to avoid and ways to prevent a repeat

  • Do not push the appliance back against the wall until the floor behind it is dry.
  • Do not run fans over a Category 2 spill without sorting out wet pad first.
  • Replace rubber washer hoses that are cracked or bulging, and consider braided ones.
  • Look under the dishwasher and behind the refrigerator when you clean, and watch for damp.
  • Know where each shutoff valve is before you need it.
