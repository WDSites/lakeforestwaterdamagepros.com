Drywall water damage repair covers the walls of your home: cutting out wet board at a safe height, drying what is behind it, and rebuilding the wall with new board, tape, texture and paint. If a wall in your Lake Forest, CA home is soft, stained or swollen, call (949) 670-0939 and we will explain whether it needs removal or can dry in place.

Walls get wet differently from ceilings. Water enters from the bottom up or from inside the cavity, so the damage often hides behind baseboards, cabinets and insulation.

How water gets into walls

Typical causes are a burst supply line, a dishwasher or washing machine failure, a leaking toilet connection, and water spreading from a flooded floor. In each case the board acts like a sponge. Water climbs the paper and gypsum by wicking, so the wet area is usually taller than the visible stain.

A wall can look dry on the surface and still be wet in the cavity. That is why moisture is measured, not judged by eye.

Warning signs a wall has taken on water

Soft or crumbly board

Gypsum has lost strength and will not dry back to firm.

Bubbled or peeling paint

Moisture is pushing outward from behind the surface.

Swollen baseboard

Wood trim has absorbed water at the bottom of the wall.

Musty smell

Something inside the cavity is staying damp.

Safety steps to take first

Shut off the water at the fixture or main valve if you can do it safely, and keep outlets in the wet area off. Do not run a household vacuum on standing water. Remove items from the wall base, and photograph the damage for your claim before anything is cut.

If the water came from a toilet overflow, sewage or a washing machine, treat it as contaminated and keep children away. The EPA says to call a professional with contaminated water experience in those cases.

Our wall repair process

1

Measure the moisture

Meters and thermal imaging show how high water has wicked and whether the cavity is wet.

2

Make the flood cut

We cut the board at a set height above the highest wet reading, with the height set by moisture readings, so the opening lands on dry material.

3

Pull baseboards and insulation

Baseboards come off carefully for reuse where possible, and wet batt insulation is bagged and removed.

4

Dry the cavity

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry studs and plates. Readings are checked daily and compared to a dry reference.

5

Rebuild the wall

New drywall is hung and taped, texture is matched, and the wall is primed and painted.

S500 water categories decide how much comes out. Category 1 clean water may allow drying in place after a small cut. Category 2 or 3 water means porous board and insulation are removed. See our water damage cleanup page for the cleaning side of the work.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while slow leaks often are not. Mold that results from a covered cause may be covered too, according to the California Department of Insurance. Check your policy and report the loss right away.

Mistakes to avoid with wet walls

  • Painting over a damp wall, which traps water and bubbles again.
  • Running a fan at the surface only, while the cavity stays wet.
  • Leaving wet insulation in place because the board looks fine.
  • Reinstalling baseboard before the wall behind it has been checked.

What Lake Forest homeowners should know about wet walls

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and the California Department of Public Health says porous materials that stay wet more than a day or two may need to be removed. Drywall and batt insulation are both porous, so if a wall was wet over a weekend, assume the answer is likely removal and not waiting.

Which water district serves your address varies, so check your bill. Irvine Ranch Water District serves most of Lake Forest, and El Toro Water District serves the southeast. If a leak came from the supply line outside your house, call the right district while you reach us.
