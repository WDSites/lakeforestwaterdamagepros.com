AC leak water damage happens when condensate from an air conditioner escapes its drain path and soaks nearby drywall, insulation or flooring. If you see a ceiling stain or a wet closet in your Lake Forest, CA home, turn the system off at the thermostat and call (949) 670-0939. The 24/7 line takes the call.

Cooling a house pulls water out of the air. That water is supposed to run out through a drain line, and when it does not, it goes wherever gravity sends it. We do not repair air conditioners, so the HVAC technician and our crew work in sequence.

Why summer water is often not rain

NOAA climate normals from the nearest airport station show June, July, August and September with almost no rain, between 0.01 and 0.10 inches each month. So a new stain that appears in midsummer is rarely a roof problem. It is far more often condensate from a cooling system running hard.

That points the search toward the air handler, the drain line and the pan. If a stain appears right after a storm in the wet season instead, the roof is a better suspect, and our page on roof leak damage covers that case.

The usual AC leak sources

Clogged condensate line

Algae and debris block the drain line, so water backs up in the pan and spills over the edge.

Overflowing drain pan

A rusted or cracked secondary pan under an attic or closet unit lets water reach the ceiling below.

Frozen evaporator coil

Low airflow or low refrigerant ices the coil. When the ice thaws, more water arrives than the pan can handle.

A stain that grows on a hot day and slows on a mild one points to condensate. A stain that only appears after rain points elsewhere. Note the pattern before you call.

Safety steps in the first hour

  1. Switch the system off at the thermostat so it stops producing water.
  2. If the ceiling is bulging or dripping, place a bucket below and keep people out from under it.
  3. Keep clear of wet light fixtures and ceiling fans, and shut off that circuit at the panel if water is near them.
  4. Photograph the stain, the unit and the pan so your insurer can see the original condition.

How we dry and repair the damage

1

Map the moisture

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera trace the water from the stain back through the ceiling and attic insulation.

2

Remove what cannot dry

Saturated drywall and wet insulation are cut out, and loose ceiling texture is scraped.

3

Dry the cavity

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match a dry reference from unaffected materials.

4

Rebuild the finish

New drywall, texture and paint are installed after the structure is dry, and flooring is repaired if it was wet.

Condensate is usually Category 1 water when it is fresh, but standing water in a pan can grow bacteria and slime. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for judging that, and the time the water sat is part of the call.

On insurance, a sudden overflow from a failed pan is often treated differently from a clogged line that dripped for months. Many policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and exclude gradual leaks. Check your policy, ask your insurer what applies and keep receipts for both the HVAC and the restoration work.

What Lake Forest homeowners should know about AC leaks

The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. An attic air handler that leaks overnight can soak insulation fast, so shutting the system off and calling the same day matters more than waiting for the HVAC visit.

If you see growth, the California Department of Public Health advises against using bleach on mold. Leave it alone and see our mold remediation page. We do not test for mold, and CDPH also advises against testing visible mold.

Prevention and mistakes to avoid

  • Have the condensate line flushed and the pan checked on the same service visit as your filter change.
  • Ask your HVAC technician about a float switch that shuts the unit off when the pan fills.
  • Do not keep running a unit that is dripping, and do not cover a stain with paint.
  • Check ceilings below attic units at the start of each cooling season.
