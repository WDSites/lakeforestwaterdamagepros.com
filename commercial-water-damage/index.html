What a commercial water loss looks like

Water gets into a commercial space from supply lines, drain backups, roof or wall leaks, and storm runoff. The first sign is often a stain, a soft ceiling tile, a musty odor or carpet that squishes underfoot. Each of those points to water that is already in the materials.

A sound ceiling tile or a dry-looking wall can hide wet insulation above or behind it. A crew checks with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera before deciding what is wet.

First-hour safety steps for the space

  1. Have a person who knows the building locate the water shutoff and close it if the source is a supply line.
  2. Keep staff, customers and visitors out of wet areas and away from sagging ceilings.
  3. Do not touch electrical equipment standing in water. Call your utility or 911 for any electrical concern.
  4. Move small, dry items and records out of the wet zone if it is safe.
  5. Photograph the damage and the source before anything is moved.

How the work runs, start to finish

1

Dispatch and assess

The same 24/7 line sends a crew who survey the damage and read moisture across the affected rooms.

2

Extract and remove

Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull standing water, and wet carpet, pad and damaged drywall are removed.

3

Dry and monitor

Air movers and LGR or desiccant dehumidifiers run, with daily readings against a dry reference, following ANSI/IICRC S500 practice.

4

Clean and rebuild

Surfaces are cleaned, and drywall, flooring and paint are rebuilt once readings are at the dry standard.

Insurance, leases and business continuity

The California Department of Insurance claims guide is written for residential property, and commercial policies differ. Ask your commercial insurer how your policy treats sudden and accidental water, what notice it needs, and what it requires you to do. We document the loss with photos, readings and drying logs for the property insurer.

Who is responsible for what, such as the building, improvements and contents, is a lease question for the owner to settle. We do not give legal advice.

For keeping a business running, be practical. Ask in the first call which rooms can stay open, which need to close during extraction and drying, and what can be done in phases. We do not promise timelines, because they depend on the extent of the water and the materials.

What Lake Forest property owners should know about the rules

Cal/OSHA regulates workplace mold, which is one more reason to dry wet materials quickly. The EPA says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours. If a worker reports a musty odor after a leak, call us about drying and ask a qualified professional about testing, since we do not do mold testing.

Building work of $500 or more, labor and materials, requires a licensed contractor under California Contractors State License Board rules. Permit needs vary by job, so ask City of Lake Forest Building and Safety Services at 949-461-3460 before rebuild work begins. Mistakes that make a commercial loss worse

  • Waiting for the property manager or insurer before calling a restoration crew.
  • Running a few fans and calling it dry while wet insulation stays behind the wall.
  • Throwing away damaged items before photographing them.
  • Rebuilding finishes before readings reach the dry standard.
