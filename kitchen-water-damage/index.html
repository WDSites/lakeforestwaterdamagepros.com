Kitchen water damage is moisture damage from leaks at the sink, dishwasher, refrigerator line or supply valves that spreads into cabinets, flooring and the subfloor. If your Lake Forest, CA kitchen has water under the sink or on the floor, shut off the local valve and call (949) 670-0939. The 24/7 line takes the call.

The kitchen packs more plumbing into one room than any other space in the house. That means more places to leak, and more finishes sitting right on top of the water.

Where kitchen water hides

Water under a sink drains toward the back wall and into the cabinet floor, which is often particleboard. From there it wicks through the toe kick and under the flooring. A leak that looks like a damp spot on the cabinet floor may have spread across the room below the surface.

Dishwasher and refrigerator supply lines leak at the connection and run behind the appliance. Nobody sees that water until it reaches the open floor or smells wrong.

Warning signs inside the kitchen

  • ✓Swollen or crumbling particleboard under the sink
  • ✓Toe kicks that bubble, peel or darken
  • ✓Laminate or hardwood planks that cup near the dishwasher
  • ✓Tile grout that stays damp or lifts at the edges
  • ✓A sour smell when you open the cabinet doors

Safety steps for the first hour

  1. Close the shutoff valve under the sink, or the main shutoff if a valve is stuck.
  2. Turn off the dishwasher and unplug small appliances only from dry outlets.
  3. Empty the cabinet under the leak so you can see where the water starts, and photograph everything.
  4. Mop standing water and keep family and pets off a wet floor.

Countertops are usually not the problem. Stone and solid-surface tops survive water fine, but they may have to be lifted so the cabinet base beneath can be dried or rebuilt. Plan for that before you decide to keep them.

How we dry a wet kitchen

1

Trace the water

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera find wet cabinet bases, flooring and wall sections behind appliances.

2

Extract and open

Portable extraction units remove standing water. Toe kicks come off and small flooring sections are lifted for airflow.

3

Dry the cavity

Air movers, injection drying through small holes and LGR dehumidifiers dry cabinet bases and subfloor, with readings logged daily.

4

Rebuild

Cabinet pieces, flooring, baseboard and paint are rebuilt after readings match a dry reference.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference for the water categories. A broken supply line is clean Category 1 water, while a dishwasher drain failure is Category 2. Sink drain backups can be worse, and that changes what we remove.

Insurance and permits for kitchen work

The California Department of Insurance guide says policyholders must take reasonable steps to prevent further damage and report the loss right away. Many policies cover sudden and accidental leaks, while gradual ones are often excluded, so check your policy and keep receipts for your shutoff and drying work.

For rebuilds, building work of $500 or more, labor and materials, requires a licensed contractor under CSLB rules. Permit needs vary by job, so ask the City of Lake Forest Building and Safety Services Division at 949-461-3460 before you start cabinet or plumbing changes.

What Lake Forest homeowners should know about kitchen leaks

The CDI guide recommends getting written bids from licensed contractors. For a kitchen, that means a bid for drying and a separate bid for the rebuild, so you can compare scopes on each.

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Cabinet bases and toe kicks hold water in enclosed space, so starting extraction the same day matters more than waiting for the plumber's schedule.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Do not seal wet cabinets behind new flooring or caulk.
  • Do not leave the dishwasher running to see if it leaks again.
  • Do not pry up laminate without photos for your insurer.
  • Do not assume a dry surface means a dry cabinet interior.
