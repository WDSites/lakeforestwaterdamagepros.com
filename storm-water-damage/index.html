How storms get water inside a Lake Forest home

Wind pushes rain sideways into window frames, door thresholds and vents that stay dry in a normal drizzle. Clogged gutters and downspouts overflow against fascia and walls. Roof flashing, tile and attic vents can let water into the attic, where it soaks insulation before stains show on the ceiling below.

Runoff can also pool at a slab edge or a garage door and come under the threshold. After a hillside downpour, mud can move with it.

Signs the storm got in

  • A drip or stain on a ceiling after rain, which can mean wet insulation above.
  • Baseboards that feel cold or damp along one wall.
  • Bubbling paint, peeling wallpaper or a musty smell in a closet.
  • Carpet that squishes near a window, patio door or garage entry.

Safety steps while the rain is still falling

Do not go on a wet roof or climb a ladder in the storm. Stay out of rooms where a ceiling is bowed, because it can drop. If water has reached outlets, fixtures or the panel, call your utility or 911 before touching anything electrical.

Put buckets under drips, move dry items and photograph the damage and the entry point. Keep out of any standing water that came from outside, since it can be contaminated.

What Lake Forest homeowners should know about storms

The county hazard plan lists floods in 1938, 1969, 1974, 1983, 1995, 1997, 2010 and 2019. It singles out a 1997 storm that caused severe damage in Lake Forest and names Serrano Creek and Borrego among areas flooded in severe storms. The practical point is that heavy storms recur here, so clear gutters and downspouts before the wet season.

The National Weather Service reported that the January 9 and 10, 2023 atmospheric river put 3 to 6 inches on Orange County foothills and 0.5 to 2 inches in the lowlands. Homes near hills can see far more runoff than nearby flat streets. OC Parks says Whiting Ranch trails may close up to 3 days after rain, a hint that water keeps moving through the foothills after the storm ends. The same applies in Rancho Santa Margarita and Laguna Hills.

Prevention before the next storm

  • Clear gutters and downspouts and point them away from the slab.
  • Check roof flashing, vents and window caulk with a roofer or handyman before November.
  • Keep soil and mulch below the stucco line and clear drains near the garage door.
  • Know where your water shutoff and electrical panel are.
