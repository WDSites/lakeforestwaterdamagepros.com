Carpet water damage restoration is the process of extracting water from carpet and pad, drying them and the subfloor, and replacing what cannot be saved. If a leak, overflow or appliance failure soaked the carpet in your Lake Forest, CA home, call (949) 670-0939 and the 24/7 line will take it.

The first question is always the same. Where did the water come from, and how long has it sat there? Those two answers decide whether the carpet stays or goes.

Why the water source decides everything

The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard sorts water into three categories. Category 1 is clean, such as a broken supply line. Category 2 is significantly contaminated, such as washing machine or dishwasher discharge. Category 3 is grossly contaminated, such as sewage or rising floodwater.

Category 1 carpet can often be dried in place along with its pad. Category 2 and 3 carpet and pad are removed, because the fibers and foam trap contaminants that surface cleaning cannot reach.

Why pad is usually replaced when contaminated

Pad is foam or fiber designed to hold air, which means it also holds water and whatever was in it. Squeezing it out never gets it clean, and it dries slowly under carpet that is still damp.

That is why pad is cheap to replace compared with the risk of leaving it. Even in Category 1 jobs, old or crumbling pad is often replaced because it will not recover its cushion.

How carpet is dried in place

1

Extract with wands

Truck-mounted or portable extraction units with weighted wands pull standing and absorbed water from the carpet and pad.

2

Lift and float

We detach the carpet at the wall and float it up off the pad on tackless edges, so air reaches both sides.

3

Air movers underneath

Air movers push air under the lifted carpet while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room.

4

Check the tack strip and subfloor

Wet tack strip and wood or concrete subfloor are metered, dried or replaced if damaged.

5

Deodorize

Once the carpet is dry, deodorizing and cleaning finish the job, and we reset the carpet.

Safety steps for the first hour

  • Stop the source. Shut the water valve or turn off the appliance.
  • Stay off wet carpet near outlets, and turn off power to the room only from a dry spot.
  • Lift furniture legs off the carpet, and put foil or plastic under them to stop rust and stains.
  • Do not use a household vacuum. Do not walk on wet carpet with dirty shoes.
  • Photograph the wet areas, then call us.

What Lake Forest homeowners should know about wet carpet

The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, and the California Department of Public Health says porous materials that stay wet more than a day or two may need to be removed. Carpet and pad are both porous, so a weekend leak you did not catch is a reason to call sooner and not wait to see.

NOAA climate normals from the nearest airport station show that the wet season runs roughly November through March. If your carpeted rooms sit near doors or low spots, check them after storms, and look at our mold remediation page if wet carpet has already begun to smell musty.

Mistakes that cost homeowners their carpet

  • Running a fan over contaminated carpet. It spreads bacteria and does not clean the pad.
  • Waiting to see if it dries. Damp pad under carpet stays wet for days.
  • Using bleach. The state health department advises against it.
  • Replacing carpet before the subfloor reads dry.
