How to Get Rid of Mold in a Basement in Lake Forest, CA

A step-by-step plan for a small patch of mold in a lower-level room, covering moisture, drying, materials, cleaning and the point where you stop.

By the Lake Forest Water Damage Pros team

To get rid of mold in a basement, you stop the water first, dry the room, remove materials that cannot be cleaned and scrub the ones that can. If your Lake Forest, CA home has a basement or lower level, this order matters more than any cleaning product.

The plan below is for a small area only. It also doubles as an answer to how to clean up mold in a basement without making the problem bigger. Each step ends with a clear point where you should stop and call instead.

Before you touch anything

Measure the patch with a tape. EPA says that if mold covers less than about 10 square feet, you can in most cases handle it yourself. Past that size, the job belongs with a professional.

Gather gloves, eye protection, a respirator rated for fine dust, a HEPA vacuum, a stiff brush, detergent, buckets and heavy trash bags. Keep children and pets out of the room. Close the door and seal any vents with tape so dust stays put.

Step one: find and fix the water

Check the usual suspects: supply lines, the water heater, the washing machine hoses, window wells, and the line where the wall meets the slab. A moisture meter helps, but a dry paper towel taped to a suspect wall overnight is a rough check too. If the towel is damp in the morning, water is still arriving.

A plumber stops a leak, and we do not do plumbing repair or waterproofing. Cleaning before the source is fixed only buys you a few weeks. Our mold remediation crews step in once the water has stopped.

Step two: dry everything within 24 to 48 hours

EPA puts it plainly: dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Move boxes and furniture off the wet floor, run a dehumidifier, point fans at the walls and open a window if the air outside is drier than the air inside.

Surface dry is not wall dry. Drywall can feel fine while the cavity behind it stays wet. When water came from a burst pipe or an appliance, a crew with air movers and a moisture meter is faster and more certain. See our emergency water removal service for active water.

Step three: decide what stays and what goes

The California Department of Public Health says porous materials that stay wet more than a day or two may need to be removed. Hard, sealed materials can usually be cleaned. When in doubt, a wet porous item that has visible growth should go in a bag.

Step four: clean the hard surfaces without spreading dust

  1. Bag removed materials in the room before carrying them out, and double-bag anything crumbly.
  2. Vacuum the surface with a HEPA vacuum first, because dry brushing sends dust into the air.
  3. Scrub with detergent and water, working top to bottom, and change the rinse water often.
  4. Wipe with clean damp rags, then dry the surface with a fan.
  5. Vacuum again once everything is dry, and put the rags in a bag.

The state health department advises against using bleach on mold, so plain detergent and water is the safer default. Do not mix cleaning products in a closed room.

Dry is the cleaning step people skip

A scrubbed wall that stays damp will grow again. Leave the fan and dehumidifier running until a moisture meter reading matches a dry wall in the same room, not until the paint looks right.

What Lake Forest homeowners should know about basement mold and the rainy months

NOAA monthly normals from the nearest airport station put the wet season at roughly November through March, with June through September almost dry. A lower-level room that picks up seepage after a winter storm is on the clock the same day, because EPA's 24 to 48 hour window starts when the material gets wet. After any heavy rain, walk the room and touch the base of the walls.

The dry summer months give you better conditions for airing out and drying, but they do not excuse a slow response. A pipe leak behaves the same in July as in February. If water has been in the room for longer than two days, treat the porous materials as suspect and plan for removal.

Step five: keep humidity from creeping back up

  • ✓Keep a hygrometer on a shelf and watch for readings that climb after rain or laundry
  • ✓Run a dehumidifier when the numbers climb, and empty the bucket or use a drain hose
  • ✓Raise stored items off the floor on plastic shelving
  • ✓Keep gutters, downspouts and soil grading directing water away from the foundation
  • ✓Check window wells and floor drains before each rainy season

Neighbors in Laguna Hills and Aliso Viejo deal with the same cycle of wet months and dry months, so a routine check helps no matter where in the area you live. Nothing here can promise mold will not return, because mold needs moisture and any new water source restarts the clock.

Stop and call a professional when

The numbers or the situation say this is no longer a small job.

  • ✓The patch is larger than about 10 square feet or keeps spreading
  • ✓Sewage, a backup or floodwater touched the area
  • ✓You smell mold but cannot find it, which can mean hidden growth
  • ✓Wet insulation or drywall covers a large wall
  • ✓The same wall grows mold again after you cleaned it

For hidden mold, EPA suggests considering an experienced professional. For sewage or other contaminated water, it says to call a professional experienced with contaminated water. Questions about symptoms belong with a physician.
