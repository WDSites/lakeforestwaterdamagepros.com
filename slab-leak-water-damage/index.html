A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs under or inside a concrete foundation slab. If your home is built on a slab, a pinhole in a supply line can soak flooring from below for weeks before anyone sees it. Call (949) 670-0939 if you suspect one in Lake Forest, CA.

How a line under concrete ends up wetting your house

Supply lines under or in a slab carry pressurized water all day. Over time a joint can loosen, a pipe wall can thin from corrosion, or the pipe can rub against rock or concrete. The water then escapes and follows the path of least resistance.

Sometimes it drains into the soil below and nothing shows. Other times it rises through cracks and joints in the slab and spreads under flooring, which is when damage appears. A hidden leak under a slab can run a long time before the signs add up.

The meter test is simple. Turn off every fixture and appliance, then watch your water meter. If the dial still spins, water is going somewhere. Ask your water utility how to read your meter and whether it can help you review usage.

Safety steps for the first hour

  1. Shut off the main water valve if the floor is wet or you hear running water, then confirm the meter stops.
  2. Turn off power to a wet room only if you can reach the panel while standing on a dry surface. Otherwise call your utility or 911.
  3. Stay off warm or soft floor areas. A hot line can scald and wet subflooring can give way.
  4. Move rugs, shoes and small furniture to dry ground, and photograph every wet area before touching it.
  5. Call a leak detection plumber and then call us for the drying plan.

Who does what, and how our crew dries the house

A leak detection plumber locates the break and repairs the line, which may mean breaking into the slab or rerouting the pipe through walls or the attic. Tear-out for access is a plumber job, and we work around it. We do not do plumbing.

Once the source is handled, we dry the flooring and structure, remove wet materials that cannot be saved, and rebuild finishes. The crew follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard for how drying goals are set.

1

Moisture mapping

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show how far water has traveled in flooring, baseboards and lower drywall.

2

Removal of wet materials

Soaked carpet, pad, laminate and baseboards come out when drying in place will not work.

3

Drying the subfloor and walls

Air movers and LGR or desiccant dehumidifiers run while we take daily moisture readings.

4

Cleaning and rebuild

Once readings match a dry reference, we clean, then replace flooring, base and drywall.

The California Department of Insurance claims guide says homeowners policies cover many kinds of water damage but not flood, and sudden and accidental losses are treated differently from slow ones. A hidden leak that ran for weeks may be treated as gradual, and coverage for the pipe repair itself can differ from coverage for the damage it caused.
